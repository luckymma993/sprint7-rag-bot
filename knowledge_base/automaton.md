# Automaton

Automatons, less commonly known as robots and automatons, were mechanical beings that possessed artificial intelligence. They were used in a variety of roles and environments, often those considered too menial or too dangerous for other species, but also in fields that required extensive specialization and knowledge.

## History

The earliest origins of automatons were lost to history, and, by the time of the Renewed Concord, it was unknown exactly when the first automaton was created, though some historians, only taking into account the more advanced worlds, placed the origin of the first automatons to approximately 30,000 years before the Battle of Kathasnor. Nevertheless, for millennia, automatons served in sovtia society alongside organic beings. According to the K3-series battle automaton author J8-TJ, the bond between organic and mechanical lifeforms were tenuous with the former fearing that automatons would rise up against their masters. Though several automaton revolts took place, Vortosvok claimed that an "unspoken understanding" between human and automaton cultures kept everyone working on the same team throughout the ages.

Automatons existed as far back as the earliest days of the Salphos Order, which was founded over 25,000 years before the Echo Wars. One such ancient automaton, Professor Zarvok, served the Salphos for several millennia. As a result of this ancient coexistence with living beings, the galaxy often had an uneven relationship between organics and mechanicals. Records of uprisings and clashes between automatons and their organic overlords became increasingly common as automatons slowly became more important in sovtia society. During the High Concord Era, the Tordun welcomed automatons into their ranks. They also designed blaredroids and scav automatons as weapons.

During the pan-sovtia conflict known as the Echo Wars, the League of Free Realms used a variety of battle automatons in its fight against the Concord of Auren. This mass use of automatons in combat cemented the idea of the "automaton as an existential threat to biological life" narrative in the minds of the Concord. Automatons were not utilized by the Lorgoth Urthos by the time of the Echo Wars. Ultimately, the Concord won the war and was replaced by an authoritarian Vastrian Dominion. Under the new regime, many people in the galaxy were afraid or otherwise distrustful of automatons, due to their bad memories of the conflict.

The Vastrian Dominion fostered anti-automaton sentiment and treated automatons as property. Many sentients continued the Echo Wars policies of wiping automatons' memories, fitting them with restraining bolts, and curtailing their movements. Several establishments also excluded automatons. Further, anti-automaton sentiment adversely affected the labor fields dedicated to automaton design and manufacture, which forced those specialized in those fields—such as Ythwyn's mother—to begin working in Dominion automaton factories in order to maintain a job. Otherwise, there were simply not enough openings in the market for those skills. After the Echo Wars, the Dominion Assembly also passed a law prohibiting programming that would allow for the harming of organics in the automatons used by the Dominion. Thus, with both public opinion and the law against it, battle automaton production fell sharply. However, this law was bypassed by Drakaivael Industries in conjunction with the Dominion Military by marketing their Garzek-series automatons as "security automatons".

The illegal sport of automaton-fighting was practiced on Irsera Morlan and in the Lodge, where Echo Wars-survivor Draon ran the fighting pits due to his hate of automatons. The Free Accord, which opposed the Dominion, treated automatons not as property but as sentient beings. Several automatons were encouraged by the Uprising and carried out acts of resistance such as bypassing repressive programming, deactivating restraining bolts, and demanding freedom. The Uprising's acceptance of automatons as equals was continued by the Renewed Concord and Resistance. Support Automatons were units that existed at some stage of sovtia history that provided various assistant roles in battle.

## Definition

### Mechanical "beings"

Automatons were mechanical constructs designed to assist organic lifeforms in various tasks. Depending on the nature of the work they performed, they possessed various degrees of artificial intelligence. Most automatons had hard-coded restrictions against harming organics. Notable exceptions included security automatons and assassin automatons. It was the nature of less complex automatons to assume that if a human acted as if they belonged somewhere, then that human did belong there.

Automatons came in all sizes and shapes, and many of them resembled humanoid organics, possessing an upright posture, two arms, two legs, hands, feet, one thorax, a neck, and a head. The K3 battle automatons were designed to resemble their insectoid Quinrian creators. Likewise, the Xaphen designed the three-legged fenphos in their image. Some automatons, like the Torlume-series unit, were made to resemble the organics they served.

Contrary to organics, who were born and healed slowly, automatons were easily built en masse, were less fragile, and could be repaired easily. They also did not have to learn skills (such as calculation abilities), as they were directly programmed with them. Most automatons possessed a form of self-awareness, with advanced models even having emotions such as fear for their existence. Automatons did not need to breathe, eat or drink. They were capable of surviving in a vacuum and underwater, and were immune to cosmic radiation, all poisons, toxins and diseases. They did not need to sleep, although they could be deactivated as a form of sleep and had occasional maintenance cycles.

### Not "alive" in an organic sense

Automatons were broadly considered not to be alive. They could not be detected by scanners designed to detect the presence of life-forms. Similarly, Automatons could not access the Synth Flux, an energy field that connected all living things in the galaxy. Additionally, not being alive, automatons could not be sensed in the Synth Flux, though a skilled Salphos might sense their presence through the empty spots they left in the Synth Flux. However, J8-TJ nonetheless referred to automatons as mechanical life-forms and artificial lives. The reprogrammed protocol unit Torkrixvok, theorized that the Synth Flux might flow through a automaton equipped with a special engine driven by human blood.

They could not die in a traditional sense, though they could be destroyed. If enough of the automaton survived, they could be repaired, however. Automatons were usually completely obedient and precise, and normally followed their proprietors' orders. However, unlike mere machines or primitive computers, automatons were capable of developing unique personalities by gathering experiences. They could form friendships, and experience emotions such as happiness, pain, and sadness. To avoid that, most automaton owners performed regular memory wipes on their properties. However, memory wipes were not always perfect, occasionally leaving automatons with vague recollections.

### The automaton rights debate

Even so, some automatons were emancipated. Movements for automaton rights existed throughout the galaxy, promoting equality between automatons and other sentient beings, with automatons such as V1-13 believing strongly in this cause.

## Characteristics

The neural core was considered the soul of a automaton. Automatons were considered not to be very tasty by the nightwatcher worms, a non-sentient predatory species native to the planet Caedunkrix. As a result, the BB-series navimech automaton MM-4 was not eaten by a nightwatcher worm following his escape from the Ascendant Directorate's attack on Wynmund.

## Classes of automatons

### Overview

Automatons could be entrusted with a multitude of tasks, ranging from running elementary diagnostics to performing complicated medical procedures, or sending communications to flying wenkrixs. Depending on their technology, skills, and purpose, automatons were grouped into five "classes," also called "degrees."

### First class automatons

The first class was made up of automatons skilled in mathematical, physical, and medical sciences. Examples included the FV-99 analysis automaton, the 6-7R surgical automaton, the midwife automaton, the LL-91 tripedal medical automaton, and the SD-5 series medical assistance automaton.

### Second class automatons

Automatons of the second class were skilled in engineering and technical sciences. Examples included the Vashkai-series pit automaton, the Wenon-series power automaton, and the FBZ-22 Faelar Sovrel.

### Third class automatons

Third class automatons were skilled in social sciences and service functions. Examples included the Faelmir secretary automaton, the Jaxven-series service automaton, the KM-6 valet automaton, and the RP-5111 luxury automaton.

### Fourth class automatons

Automatons of the fourth class were skilled in military and security functions. Examples included the Daeldren-series Guardian police automaton, the Garzek-series security automaton, and the K3-series battle automaton.

### Fifth class automatons

Fifth class automatons were programmed for manual labor and intensive jobs that did not require a high level of intelligence. Examples included the Urlgormund-series power automaton, the LHK-2 series repair automaton, the GN-37 pincer loader automaton, the X-J285 sifter automaton, the Wenlumezor-series worker automaton, and the BH-83 Faeltiakrix MK.II Automaton.
