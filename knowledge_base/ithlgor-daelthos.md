# Ithlgor Daelthos

Ithlgor Daelthos, also known simply as Ithlgor, was a Salphos member of the Resistance during the war against the Ascendant Directorate. The human woman was a scavenger on the planet Caedunkrix, but her life was changed by tumultuous events in the last days of the Renewed Concord Era. Ithlgor's decision to help the automaton MM-4 set her on a course to discovering the Synth Flux awakening within her, leading her to seek out Salphos Master Havrgan Daelthos for training. Despite her commitment to the Salphos Order and the radiant side of the Synth Flux, Ithlgor felt the pull to the umbral side due to her anger as well as the adversarial relationship she had with Zunria Sallgor, a fallen Salphos who joined the Ascendant Directorate as a warrior named Rhorn Daelven; they shared a unique bond as a dyad in the Synth Flux.

Ithlgor was born on Fenthas in 15 AKV during the rise of the Renewed Concord. Her father, Baelrnvael, was a bioengineered Strand-Cast cloned from the genetic template of Ursera Bregoth, the Zargoth Lord who ruled the Vastrian Dominion as Emperor Vorix until his death and resurrection following the Battle of Wynthas. The clone and his wife, Dramirthil, chose to live as simple junk traders in order to hide their daughter, who inherited Bregoth' connection to the Synth Flux, from his followers. Even after the deaths of her parents, Ithlgor was unaware of her Vorix bloodline and was forced to survive on her own in the Caedunkrix desert, and became a skilled mechanic, pilot, and combatant in the process. Her isolated life was disrupted shortly before war broke out across the galaxy in 34 AKV between the Ascendant Directorate and the Resistance. Determined to help the Resistance, Ithlgor gained several new allies including Wynthil, a renegade legionnaire, as well as the veterans Ophsera Sallgor and Lordris aboard the Silver Kestrel. In the course of their journey, she discovered her latent Flux powers and sought guidance under Havrgan Daelthos, who taught her the art of the Synth Flux despite his decision to end the Salphos legacy. After Daelthos sacrificed himself to save the Resistance, Ithlgor assumed his mantle as the last Salphos and became the apprentice of General Vegoth Faelvok.

With the return of Ursera Bregoth in 35 AKV, Ithlgor set out to find the dark world of Baellan and prevent the Zargoth from being reborn through the resurrected Dark Lord. It was through Rhorn Daelven that Ithlgor discovered her origins as Vorix's granddaughter, and upon confronting the resurrected Emperor in person, she was nearly swayed to the umbral side of the Synth Flux, thus falling into his trap of transferring his spirit into her, which will allow him to possess her body. However, Ithlgor was saved by Zunria Sallgor after he renounced his dark alter ego. Aided by the spirits of all the Salphos, Ithlgor died vanquishing her grandfather, causing Sallgor to make the ultimate sacrifice in order to bring Ithlgor back to life. The two embraced and shared a kiss as Sallgor peacefully vanished in Ithlgor's arms. Although Sallgor was the last of the Daelthos bloodline, Ithlgor assumed the name "Daelthos" to honor their memory and in recognition of her chosen family. Sometime later, the now self-proclaimed Ithlgor Daelthos became a Salphos Master.

## Biography

### Itheth-birth

In 1 BKV, during the Great Schism War and more than a decade before Ithlgor's birth, her voice was heard by Novice Vezek Cyrnor during his brief visit to the World Between Worlds—a place that existed beyond time and space.

Ithlgor and Zunria Sallgor were the culmination of a prophesied Flux dyad as well as the Rule of Two. Inspired by the Doctrine of the Torlan, the Rule of Two mandated that only two Zargoth could exist at a given time, a Zargoth Master and a Zargoth apprentice, though its ultimate goal was to create a dyad between two Zargoth Lords. Ursera Saldun tried to create a dyad with his apprentice, Ursera Bregoth, who, in turn, tried to create one with Sallgor's grandfather, Yarwyn Daelthos. Despite years of searching, the Zargoth never found a way to form a dyad on their own. Bregoth regarded the Rule of Two as a pale imitation and an unworthy but necessary successor to the older, purer doctrine of the dyad that would be ultimately fulfilled by Sallgor and Ithlgor.

### Early life

#### Hunted by the Zargoth

Born on the planet Fenthas in 15 AKV, Ithlgor was the daughter of Baelrnvael and Dramirthil. While their names were lost to history, Ithlgor's father was the cloned son—an artificial genetic strandcast—of the resurrected Ursera Bregoth, making her the granddaughter of the fallen Sovtia Emperor and Dark Lord of the Zargoth. Although Bregoth had not anticipated his son's marriage with Dramirthil, he sought to claim their child who, unlike his son, had a strong connection to the Synth Flux. As such, Ithlgor was a receptacle strong enough to hold her grandfather's spirit, which was trapped in an imperfect cloned body. To protect Ithlgor from her grandfather, her parents escaped into hiding, choosing to become ordinary junk traders to keep their daughter safe.

Ithlgor's parents had a difficult life; they struggled to survive on the desert planet Caedunkrix while raising the daughter whom they loved. Since the Emperor's son and his wife absconded with their child, the Zargoth cultist Gisvok, on orders from Bregoth himself, endeavored to find the strandcast and his daughter. Ithlgor's grandfather had foreseen what she had the capability of becoming, recognizing her immense strength in the Synth Flux, and that such power was not mere ordinary power. It was his own power. Because of this, Bregoth was determined to make certain that one day, Ithlgor would embrace her destiny as a Vorix, and of course, the heir of his Zargoth legacy and successor as Sovtia Emperor. At the same time, two Accord heroes, Salphos Master Havrgan Daelthos and former General Wendak Daelvael, began investigating a disturbance in the Synth Flux after Daelvael had overheard a drunk Gisvok discussing his orders to kidnap the young Ithlgor. However, the two never learned the girl's name nor saw the family face-to-face.

Ultimately, Ithlgor's parents returned to Caedunkrix in Gisvok's own ship, the Zunlume Legacy, and, trusting that he would look out for his self-interest, hired Marar Zelgan to hide her within Fylgor Ithvok until they returned for her. Above the planet, they were captured by Gisvok; refusing to give up their daughter's location, they were both killed by the hunter on the Emperor's order. Despite telling herself they lived, Ithlgor, deep down, was left with the belief that their bodies were buried in the Caedunkrix desert, in a paupers' grave, and a suspicion that they had sold her for drinking money. In truth, they were buried by Daelvael and Daelthos on the planet Bremund. Tricked into traveling to the planet Cyrdris because Dramirthil took some Thayn beads from Zelgan in case they were caught, Gisvok never succeeded in finding Ithlgor and died within Cyrdris's Forbidden Valley.

Since Ithlgor's parents never returned to end their arrangement with Zelgan, he considered Ithlgor in debt to him and required her to work for him to pay it back. With Daelthos and Daelvael unaware of her hiding place, Ithlgor was forced to grow up alone as a result of her parents' deaths. She endured years of isolation while waiting to be reunited with her parents, having subconsciously suppressed the memory of what she knew to be true—they were no one, and they were dead. Nevertheless, Ithlgor remained hopeful that she would see them again. She recalled voices from her childhood, referring to her as "sweetheart" and promising to return for her one day. Ithlgor had believed that they were her parents, though she was not certain if the promise was made by her mother, her father, or both.

Throughout her life on Caedunkrix, she would sometimes see off-worlders in Fylgor Ithvok, a chief trade ithvok on the planet, who she thought might have arrived on Caedunkrix accidentally, unaware of the harsh realities of the desert world. Ithlgor wondered if she was left behind under similar circumstances, if her family had come to Caedunkrix by accident and she was left behind as a result of their misfortune. Whatever the reasons were, she held out hope that her family would return for her one day, though she began to realize that they would never come back. She proved unwilling to admit this even to herself for a long time.
