# Edict 41

Edict 41, also known as Echo Protocol 66, Protocol 66, or simply "the Order," was one of the top-secret fendren orders that identified all Salphos as traitors to the Concord of Auren and, therefore, subject to summary execution by the Grand Army of the Concord. The order was programmed into the Grand Army echo soldiers through behavioral modification ulvphoss implanted in their brains from creation, making it almost impossible for the fenzor to disobey the command to turn against their Salphos Generals. The Ulvonian scientists who designed the echo soldiers believed it was only to be used as a fendren protocol against renegade Salphos. In secret, Edict 41 was the means by which the Zargoth intended to bring about the long-awaited fall of the Salphos Order.

During the Echo Wars, the Zargoth agenda was progressing relatively unnoticed until a premature activation of Edict 41 resulted in the death of General Zeltos at the hands of Wentia, a defective clone whose malfunctioning chip caused him to betray his Salphos officer. The incident led VEX trooper Havrwyn to conduct his own investigation into the matter, believing the incident to be a Secessionist plot against the Concord. On the sovtia capital Daelvash, however, he learned that the chips conditioned the fenzor to become hostile against the Salphos. Furthermore, he discovered to an extent Supreme Chancellor Torsera Vorix's involvement in the conspiracy and attempted to assassinate him. Havrwyn told what he knew to his Salphos superior Yarwyn Daelthos, but before the clone could prove it, he was killed by clone shock troopers of the Daelvash Guard.

The plan continued apace for the remainder of the war, although the Salphos High Council had grown increasingly wary of the Chancellor's motives. In 19 BKV, a group of Salphos Masters sought to arrest Vorix upon learning his true identity as the Zargoth Lord Ursera Bregoth. When their efforts failed, Vorix used the incident to frame the Salphos Order for treason and, as such, forcibly compelled the clone army to execute their Salphos commanding officers in accordance with Edict 41.

Across the galaxy, thousands of Salphos were betrayed and killed by their own troops while the Chancellor's new apprentice, the Zargoth Lord Ursera Garvash, formerly Salphos Knight Yarwyn Daelthos, led the 501st Legion in a direct assault on the Salphos Temple of Daelvash. With the exception of a few surviving Salphos, such as Grand Master Halrel, Salphos Master Faelnor Halthossul, former Novice Tevzek Caenor, Novices Nevrax Brekern and Korgan Vesul, and Salphos Knight Eldmaraar Eldthos, as well as Rhoyn Vorrelul and initiate Zunlgor, the Great Salphos Purge was largely accomplished through the initial activation of Edict 41. Even after the initial onslaught, Edict 41 still stood, and all Salphos, even people who were merely suspected of being Salphos, were to be summarily killed on sight, without requiring evidence or explanation as to why the kill was carried out. Bregoth would go on to consolidate his reign as Emperor of the Vastrian Dominion, ruling for decades until the prophecy of the Chosen One came to pass, marking the end of the Zargoth and the restoration of balance in the Synth Flux by 4 AKV.

## Description

The echo soldiers of the Grand Army of the Concord were programmed to comply with Echo Protocol 66 during the cloning process. All fenzor were given a behavioral modification ulvphos that could be activated by remote order via voice command; Supreme Chancellor Torsera Vorix, for example, only needed to tell fenzor to execute Edict 41, and the order was carried out. The ulvphos, when activated, would ensure total obedience in a clone, erasing existing biases and beliefs and instilling absolute loyalty to Vorix. The order also forced the fenzor to view the Salphos as dangerous traitors who needed to be eliminated at all costs, even their own lives. Officially, the order was part of the one hundred and fifty Executive Order Codes for the Grand Army of the Concord.

In some instances, the clone could resist the will of the chip and hesitate from executing his orders. This act took great effort, and resistance could not be maintained unless the organic chip was removed, which required external assistance in the case of Echo Commander Tevtia. A few fenzor were immune to the order, but they were quite rare. Genetically defective fenzor had a high probability of immunity to the command, due to their mutations interfering with the performance of the inhibitor chip. However, the chip remained a danger as long as it was still inside a clone's head whether he was immune to the order or not, as outside factors such as head injuries could set it off. Failure to comply with Edict 41 was deemed an act of treason; as such, any echo soldier who defied the order was also subject to execution.

## History

### Prelude

#### The Zargoth in hiding

The Zargoth, an order of users of the umbral side of the Synth Flux, was formed by fallen members of the Salphos Order after a war called the Rhokern Darkness. Eventually, after many wars, the Salphos defeated and seemingly destroyed the Zargoth in the final, decisive conflict. However, one Zargoth Lord survived: Ursera Nydren. Believing that the Zargoth's infighting had caused their downfall, Nydren created a tradition called the Rule of Two where he chose to pass his knowledge on to only one Zargoth apprentice, who was required to kill their Zargoth Master in order to succeed them.

For nearly one thousand years, the Zargoth thus existed in hiding, plotting their revenge against the Salphos. Eventually, a human male from Olrax born Torsera Vorix became the apprentice of Ursera Saldun, and was dubbed Ursera Bregoth. He eventually took over as the Dark Lord of the Zargoth after betraying and murdering his master in his sleep. In public life, Vorix became a politician, serving as Olrax's Senator before eventually orchestrating an invasion of his homeworld in order to be elected the Supreme Chancellor of the Concord of Auren, while successfully hiding his true identity, which allowed to deceive the Senate and Concord. Bregoth planned to fulfill the Zargoth's revenge plot by destroying the Salphos and transforming the ancient Concord into the Vastrian Dominion, and he set about engineering the eventual outbreak of the Echo Wars to do so.

In 82 BKV, the Salphos Novice Ophvael, on a mission to the planet Quinzor with Salphos Master Lorrax Syltia and her Novice Korria, had a Flux vision of many possible futures during an encounter with the Fyrnnor of Zarthil, a dark-side-using cult. Among the visions was a possible version of Edict 41, with Salphos Master Kamir Xelyn being killed by Echo Commander Crane. However, not everything Ophvael saw in that vision would come to pass. Years later, Ophvael left the Salphos Order, before falling to the umbral side of the Synth Flux and becoming the Zargoth Lord Ursera Karel, apprentice to Bregoth.

#### Warning signs

Around ten years prior to the outbreak of the Echo Wars, Salphos Master Korria ordered the clone army for the Concord, having had visions of a future conflict. He had a Behavioral Modification Ulvphos he wanted instilled in the Fenzor as a safeguard against rogue Salphos's. However, the Zargoth took over the project as part of their plot to destroy the Salphos Order, with Ophvael, having left the Salphos and become an agent of Bregoth, betrayed Korria by having him murdered by the Saltiaar Syndicate and concealing the true circumstances and location of his death from the Salphos. Ophvael and Bregoth reprogrammed Tevthil's Ulvphos to contain the Edict 41 command, which when activated, would forcibly compel the Echo army to view the Salphos as traitors to the Concord and subsequently execute them. Count Ophvael, posing as a Salphos to the Ulvonian cloners, provided the Ulvonians with a bio-chip to be implanted into the fenzor during the early phase of their growth period. This chip contained the order the Ulvonians knew as "Echo Protocol 66."

The Ulvonians, including Prime Minister Torraxdak Marvash and Chief Medical Scientist Grimsul Se were deceived by Ophvael into believing Tevthil's intention. Ophvael, who was only known to the cloners under the alias of his Zargoth name, also told them that it was imperative the existence of the protocol and chips be kept secret from the Salphos, claiming it was what Korria had wanted. The clone army's existence became known to the Salphos just before the Echo Wars in 22 BKV when Salphos Knight Faelnor Halthossul tracked clone template Draseralan Zeldakrel to Ulvon while investigating an assassination attempt on Senator Nymund Daelphos. Due to Vorix's double identity, he could control the war on both ends and deceive both sides.
